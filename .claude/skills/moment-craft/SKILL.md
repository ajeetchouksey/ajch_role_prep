# Moment craft

A sub-topic is a short interview: 4 to 6 moments that test different decisions
about one subject, in an order that builds.

## Planning a series

Usual shape: **know-cold** (the opener) → **question** (the core decision) →
**pushback** (defend it) → **challenge** (apply it to a messy scenario) →
**question** (prove it worked). Each moment tests a different decision; write
the decision down before writing the moment.

## Know-cold

The answer to "walk me through X". `points` are the 3 to 5 things a strong
60-second answer must mention; `short` is that answer, spoken, at the level.
The Leadership version answers "in plain terms, why does this matter?"

## Question

- `asked`: how this level's interviewer phrases it.
- `testing`: one sentence on what the interviewer is really checking.
- `structure`: 3 to 5 short steps the strong answer follows, in order.
- `strong`: about 2 minutes spoken, first person, following the structure.
- `weak` and `weakWhy`: see `rubric-craft`.
- `say` and `avoid`: short quotable lines; these become the night-before sheet.

## Pushback

The interviewer challenges the answer just given. Three options: one strong,
two weak or one weak and one partial.

**Giveaway rules** (enforced by `scripts/check-options.mjs`):
1. The strong option is 0.7x to 1.3x the mean length of the others. Give wrong
   options the same depth: a reason, a consequence, a plan.
2. Wrong options contain no absolutes (always, never, guaranteed, "cannot ... at all").
3. Each wrong option is a real temptation: conceding without evidence, deciding
   by authority, following a trend, a blanket policy.

`why` explains each verdict in one sentence; `takeaway` is the principle to
remember for any pushback of this type.

## Challenge

A realistic scenario with numbers (a 200-question audit, 15% wrong citations,
two weeks). `frame` is 3 to 5 steps of a good answer; the `model` answer
follows the frame; `followUp` is the twist that tests judgement ("wrong
citations drop to 4% but p95 latency doubled"). The scenario is shared, and the
question and rubric change by level.

## Order and difficulty

Start where a prepared candidate can win, so the first try produces a visible
improvement. Put the hardest judgement call late.
